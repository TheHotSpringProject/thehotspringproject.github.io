About this map

This map was inspired by NOAA's former hot springs viewer, which the National Geophysical Data Center ran until it was retired in 2025. It shows all 1,661 thermal springs in the Thermal Springs List for the United States, compiled by Berry, Grim and Ikelman for NOAA in 1980, plus thousands more springs, hot to cool, from state geological surveys and the Water Quality Portal, and about 90 Canadian springs from the Yukon Geological Survey, Natural Resources Canada's place names and OpenStreetMap, with newer temperature readings and more precise locations where those sources have them. A separate Named springs layer (in the legend) adds about 33,000 springs from USGS place names. Use it to find a warm soak near your next trip, see which springs sit on public land, and jump straight to directions.

What you can do

  • Browse springs on topo, satellite, street or shaded-relief maps.
  • Filter by name, state, minimum temperature and land ownership, or sort by distance from you.
  • Tap a spring for its temperature, location, land manager, photos and a description, with a one-tap link to Google Maps.

Where the information comes from

  • Springs: NOAA Key to Geophysical Records Doc. No. 12 (1980), official copy at the NOAA Institutional Repository.
  • State survey springs, readings and locations: state geological surveys via the National Geothermal Data System, republished as AASG-GDS (Zaffos and Richard, 2025, doi:10.5281/zenodo.8125526, CC BY 4.0). Each spring lists its original source.
  • Nevada springs and readings: Nevada Bureau of Mines and Geology geothermal database (Hot Springs and Wells), with each record's original reference.
  • Canadian springs: Yukon Geological Survey Geothermal Thermal Springs (names and measured temperatures for the Yukon, northern BC and the western NWT; Open Government Licence – Yukon), the Canadian Geographical Names Database (Natural Resources Canada; Open Government Licence – Canada) and OpenStreetMap hot springs (© OpenStreetMap contributors, ODbL). Banff Upper Hot Springs and Cave and Basin are placed by hand. There is no land-ownership layer for Canada, and many Canadian springs have no published temperature yet.
  • New Mexico springs and readings: New Mexico Bureau of Geology and Mineral Resources, springs discharging above 30 °C (Kelley, 2015, doi:10.15121/1223849, CC BY 4.0).
  • Spring temperatures: the Water Quality Portal (USGS, EPA, National Park Service and other agencies), newest reading at each spring site.
  • Soaking estimate (springs 90 °F and warmer): this app's own judgement of whether people soak there. Known soaking spots include both commercial ones (resorts, pools, bathhouses) and wild ones (undeveloped pools people use); other springs are marked possibly used, unlikely or not allowed, or no evidence found. It is based on widely known soaking spots, state-by-state web searches, Google Maps place listings, Wikipedia, land status and temperature, and is a guide only: conditions, access and rules change.
  • Named springs layer: U.S. Geological Survey, Geographic Names Information System (feature class Spring).
  • Land ownership: USGS Protected Areas Database (PAD-US 4).
  • Descriptions & photos: Wikipedia, Wikimedia Commons and Google Maps contributors, credited on each spring.
  • Maps: USGS, Esri, OpenTopoMap and OpenStreetMap contributors.

Get the app

Install this map on your phone for a home-screen icon, full-screen view and offline use. iPhone/iPad: open the site in Safari, tap Share, then Add to Home Screen. Android/Chrome: tap Install app below or in the browser menu.

Much of this data is decades old and locations can be off. Many springs are on private or closed land, so check access and conditions before you go, and leave no trace.
