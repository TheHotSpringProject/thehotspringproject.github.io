It uses publicly released government data; the official sources are linked at the end of this description and on the app's About page.

Find a hot spring for your next soak, road trip, or geology outing. The Hot Spring Project maps about 6,000 thermal springs across North America, from Alaska and the Yukon to New Mexico, each colored by water temperature on a smooth scale from cool blue to near-boiling red.

FIND A PLACE TO SOAK
• Known soaking spots stand out: a house icon for commercial ones (resorts, pools, bathhouses) and a hot-spring icon for wild ones
• Filter to commercial spots, wild spots, or both

EVERYTHING ABOUT A SPRING
• Tap any spring for its temperature, newer readings where available, location, land manager, description, and photos
• One tap opens directions

SORT AND FILTER EVERY SPRING
• Search by name, or filter by state, land ownership, and soaking estimate
• Set a minimum temperature anywhere from 80°F to 212°F
• Sort hottest, coolest, or nearest to you (your location stays on your device)
• Expand the results into a full table

MAPS FOR EVERY TRIP
• Dark map, light USGS topo map, or satellite imagery, with roads and place names on the dark and satellite maps
• Save map areas to use offline where there's no signal
• Turn on about 33,000 more named springs from USGS place names
• Save springs and mark the ones you've visited
• Works on phones, tablets, and computers, in °F or °C

The soaking estimate is the app's own judgement from public information. Always check access, safety, and current rules before you go: many springs are on private land, some are closed, and some are hot enough to scald.
